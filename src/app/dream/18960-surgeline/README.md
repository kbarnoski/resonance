# Surgeline

**Status**: demoable — raw-WebGL2 stormglass journey-engine; leaky-integrator tension arc from slow/fast body-motion bands; real-catalog vertical layering through safeMaster; live pose / pointer / autonomous-demo all drive the identical chain. Builds clean and passes `qa-proto`. Live-camera feel unverifiable in the build sandbox (no webcam).

What if your body's motion dynamics composed the energy ARC of one of Karel's real piano recordings over minutes — a journey-engine where sustained broad movement BUILDS the piece and stillness lets it recede? Surgeline is not another instrument. The headline is the journey: a single tension envelope that is earned slowly and drains gently, so minute three is a materially bigger sonic and visual space than minute zero. The camera reads your seated upper body as velocity, not position; a leaky integrator turns sustained effort into a long climb and rest into a slow recession.

Body-motion dynamics drive the arc through a slow/fast band split. Per-frame displacement of the shoulders and wrists becomes an instantaneous motion energy (velocity). That energy is decomposed into a SLOW band — a heavily smoothed whole-body energy, ~0.8 s time constant — and a FAST band — the high-pass flicker of quick gestures, ~0.12 s. The slow band feeds the integrator (`arc += max(0, slow − threshold) · fillRate · dt; arc −= leakRate · (0.4 + arc) · dt`, clamped to [0,1]), so broad sustained motion fills the arc faster than it leaks while you keep moving. The fast band never touches the long arc; it sparks momentary articulation. Tracking is gated only on the shoulders (the torso reference is the shoulder midpoint) with wrists contributing to energy while visible — never on hips or legs — so a seated, waist-up webcam is enough.

The research anchor is Dyna2Music, "Where the Body Keeps the Beat: Structured Motion Conditioning and Music Dynamics Supervision for Dance-to-Music Generation" (arXiv:2610.00726, submitted 2026-09-30). Its core idea is to decompose body-joint velocities into slow and fast components and to supervise the generated music's DYNAMICS — its frame-to-frame rate of change — with them. Under the lab's absolute rule (rule 10: we never generate audio, we only transform Karel's real recording) the idea is INVERTED: the same slow/fast velocity decomposition instead conducts a transformation of his existing piano take. Slow whole-body energy drives the slow-varying tension envelope (the long-form build); fast energy drives the fast-varying articulation.

The audio is one real buffer, layered vertically, all terminating at the shared safe-master bus (never `ctx.destination`). A dry base voice plays through a lowpass that opens from ~700 Hz to ~13 kHz as the arc rises; a reverb-wet voice runs through a convolver whose impulse response is a windowed, exponentially-decaying slice of the SAME buffer, so the hall is literally made of his piano; a half-speed sub voice adds weight through the middle of the journey; a double-speed, high-shelf-lifted shimmer voice crowns the crest. Each voice's gain is a smoothstep of the arc, ramped about a second via `setTargetAtTime`. Fast-motion articulation briefly brightens the base cutoff and fires a short Hann-windowed grain tap of his own buffer. There are no oscillators, no synths, no noise. The picture is a raw WebGL2 fragment field in a "stormglass" palette — graphite-black ground, a deep oceanic blue-green current that brightens to a pale luminous jade crest, with a thin warm-amber rimlight only near the climax — driven from the master analyser, with a Canvas2D fallback that mirrors the same palette and arc on context loss or missing WebGL2.

Honest note on what could NOT be verified: there is no webcam in the build environment, so the live-camera feel is reasoned-through and demo-tested, not confirmed against a human body. In particular, whether the slow/fast thresholds are genuinely comfortable for a seated person, and whether the arc reads as a satisfying multi-minute build in a real room, remain untested. The autonomous demo (`demo · autonomous`) and the pointer fallback (`pointer · motion`) both drive the identical arc, audio and visual chain — climbing through a build, holding near peak, then receding — so the pipeline is complete and alive on load; only the ergonomics of real motion are unverified.
