# 21920-pitchlattice — Pitch / Time Lattice

**Status**: demoable

What if you could transpose Karel's piano's *pitch* independently of its *time* — slow one of his recordings to a glacial drone without dropping the register, or stretch it with no chipmunk effect — and fly through a stark 3-D datamatics lattice that lets you *see* the two axes come apart? Move the mouse left/right to transpose ±12 semitones; move up/down to set the time rate from four times slower to twice as fast. Slide sideways only and the register changes while the tempo holds; slide up/down only and the tempo changes while the register holds. That split is the whole piece.

The sound engine decouples pitch from time with two stages. First, varispeed: `source.playbackRate` is the **time axis** (0.25×–2.0×), but varispeed drags pitch along with it as a side effect. Second, a **time-domain overlap-add delay-line pitch shifter** applies a compensating ratio `ρ = desiredPitchRatio / timeFactor`, cancelling the varispeed's pitch change and imposing the chosen pitch — so the output register equals `desiredPitchRatio` regardless of the time rate. When `ρ ≈ 1` the shifter is transparent and the readout honestly says "coupled (varispeed)"; otherwise it reads "decoupled". The shifter is two `DelayNode`s whose `delayTime` is swept with a linear **sawtooth** (a ramping delay reads the buffer at a shifted rate), each gated by a raised-cosine (Hann) crossfade and offset by half a window so one tap is at full while the other wraps — the Hann pair sums to constant amplitude, so the sawtooth discontinuity is inaudible. Everything is scheduled automation (`setValueAtTime` + `linearRampToValueAtTime` for the sawtooth, `setValueCurveAtTime` for the envelopes) topped up on a small lookahead scheduler; there is no synth node and no LFO node of any kind, and the only sink is `createSafeMaster(ctx).input`.

The visual is a `THREE.InstancedMesh` lattice you fly through: **X = pitch** (semitone columns −12…+12), **Z = time/depth** (the scroll), **Y = register/amplitude** seeded from his note roll (`loadTrackAnalysis`; an even raster keyed to the buffer length when analysis is null — the readout reports `notes` vs `grid`). The lattice slides along X as you transpose and scrolls along Z at the playback rate, so the two axes literally move in different directions. It is Ikeda-flavoured datamatics: a lifted near-black ground, white cells, red for the active pitch column and the ringing cells, hard quantized brightness steps, `NormalBlending`, `depthWrite` for real occlusion, `FogExp2` haze, a size-attenuating perspective camera — no bloom, no glow, no overlay. It is fully playable with the mouse alone (arrow keys and space too); an optional one-hand camera is a labelled secondary override that drives the same cursor and widens the active band on an open hand, degrading to the pointer on any failure.

References, honestly: the time-domain overlap-add delay-line pitch shifter — two cross-faded, sawtooth-swept delay taps — as in Zölzer (ed.), *DAFX: Digital Audio Effects*, and Dodge & Jerse, *Computer Music*. The pitch/time decoupling is the principle a phase vocoder reaches by frequency-domain means; here it is done entirely in the time domain with Web Audio nodes. The visual register follows Ryoji Ikeda's *datamatics* data-raster work, read into 3-D.
