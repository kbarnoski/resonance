# Particle Engine

**Status**: demoable — SHOWCASE cut for one decision (lead visual: real particles vs shader-drawn particles); lab UI at `?lab=1`. Resonance's first REAL GPU particle system (WebGL2 GPGPU, 262k–1M particles at native DPR), each particle driven by its own FFT band of Karel's recording; four souls; luminance governor; reusable engine at `src/lib/particles/` ready to be cast as a journey lead-actor layer. Passes `qa-proto`.

**The showcase (default).** One question for Karel: *do I want this crisp, sound-driven particle look as the lead visual in journeys, instead of shader-drawn particles?* Pure black, no controls. Tap once: "Rebound" plays (chosen by measurement — the catalog's strongest bass and high end) and the field flows Vortex → split → Smoke of Light → Fibonacci Bloom → Murmuration (~50 s each, 8 s blends), with a small caption at each change. During the split the left half is today's shader lead (`galaxy-seed`, the Kinetic Lab lead, rendered exactly as the journey visualizer does: WebGL1, 1.5× DPR ceiling, band values × 0.85) on the same audio; the right half is the particle Vortex. Press **C** to toggle the comparison any time. `?lab=1` restores the full lab UI (souls, density, take picker, readouts).

What if every mote of light were a real simulated body listening to its own slice of the piano? Every visual layer in the journeys today is a full-screen fragment shader: "particles" are faked per pixel (loops over a few dozen distance fields) and the music reaches them through a handful of uniforms. This prototype is the other technique — a true particle engine. Up to a million bodies live in float textures on the GPU; a simulation pass integrates every one of them each frame, and a draw pass renders them as crisp, soft-edged sprites at native device-pixel ratio, additive on pure black.

**How the music gets in.** The tamed master tap is read as a 4096-point FFT and folded into 128 log-spaced bands (30 Hz – 12 kHz) with per-band automatic gain, so a quiet passage still moves the field. Each band carries a level and a kinetic *drive* — the deviation from its own ~400 ms average, slewed so motion swells rather than jitters (the same law as the journey shaders' kinetic EQ, sharing `BAND_PROFILES` time-dilation ranges). The 128 bands are uploaded every frame as a small texture; each particle owns a band and samples it. Bass motes surge their orbit radius and dilate their own clock; mid motes ride a curl-noise current whose strength follows their band; the finest treble dust shimmers and scatters. Spectral-flux onset detection with an adaptive threshold opens slow *swells*.

**Never a flash.** The music drives motion and structure — never brightness. Particle energy is constant; a luminance governor downsamples every few frames (async read-back, no stall) and lets global exposure drift by at most 2% per update, so density changes can only glide (WCAG 2.3.1).

**The souls.** *Vortex* — a three-arm log-spiral galaxy that breathes with the bass. *Smoke of Light* — a rising curl-noise current with long trails. *Fibonacci Bloom* — 4,096 golden-angle florets that unfurl on swells, a bass ripple rolling outward. *Murmuration* — a lagged ribbon of light chasing a wandering leader, folding as it turns. Switching souls blends the force laws over several seconds: the particles flow into the new form, never cut. Each soul's palette is swappable so it can be cast into any journey's colours.

**Probe.** `window.__resonanceParticles.stats()` returns fps (mean and 5th percentile), particle count, band drives/levels, swell, exposure, measured mean luminance and its largest step, and a sampled particle state (mean speed and radius by band) read back from the GPU. URL params: `?n=` particle count, `?soul=`, `?dpr=`, `?auto=0`.
