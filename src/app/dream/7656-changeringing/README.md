# 7656 · Change Ringing

`state: ritual / meditative-trance · pole: cosmic-ambient`

## The one question

What if you could fall into the hypnotic trance of English change-ringing — an
ancient combinatorial ritual where a set of bells rings *every permutation of its
order exactly once*, never repeating a row until it returns home — rendered as a
slowly-woven mandala-braid you can watch and hear forever?

## The technique — Plain Bob, from scratch

This is a real change-ringing **method**, implemented with no dependencies
(`ringing.ts`). Change-ringing rings a set of tuned bells through a sequence of
*changes* (rows); each row is a permutation of the previous one, produced by the
method's **place notation**. This piece rings **Plain Bob** — the oldest and
simplest method — on **6 bells (Plain Bob Minor)** or **7 (Plain Bob Triples)**.

**Reference:** Fabian Stedman, *Tintinnalogia* (1668) and *Campanalogia* (1677) —
the origins of change-ringing method.

### The algorithm

- Start from **rounds**: bells in home order `1 2 3 … N`.
- Each change is generated by a place-notation rule. For even stages (Minor) Plain
  Bob alternates the **cross** change `x` (every adjacent pair swaps) with `1N`
  (bells in 1st and Nth place stay; the rest swap in pairs). For odd stages
  (Triples) it alternates the back place `7` with the front place `1`. Every lead
  ends with a **lead-end** change — `12` for a plain lead, `14` for a **bob**
  (`127` / `147` on odd stages).
- A **plain course** (all plain leads) runs `2·N·(N−1)` changes — **60 rows** for
  Minor, **84** for Triples — cycling through that many *distinct* rows and then
  returning home to rounds, never repeating a row until it does. A **bob touch**
  (a bob called at every lead) is a different, shorter path that also returns to
  rounds.

### Correctness

The engine was verified by logic and simulation before shipping:

- **Every row is a genuine permutation** — `applyChange` only ever swaps disjoint
  adjacent pairs and holds the "made" places, so no two bells can ever share a
  place. Verified across all rows of a course for stages 6, 7 and 8.
- **It returns home.** The plain course comes round in exactly `N−1` leads with
  all rows distinct (60 distinct rows for Minor, 84 for Triples — no duplicate
  before rounds). The bob-every-lead touch comes round in 3 leads (Minor) / 4
  (Triples), all rows valid permutations throughout.

## The blue-line braid (visual)

The visual is the ringers' famous **"blue line"**: each bell's *position* over
successive rows, traced as a continuous polyline down the field. Because every row
is a permutation, the lines weave over and under one another into a symmetric,
non-repeating braid. It scrolls slowly upward so it feels infinite.

- **Inline SVG only** — no canvas, no WebGL, no WebGPU. Each bell is drawn as a
  crisp polyline plus a wide, low-opacity "glow" polyline underneath (a cheap
  filterless bloom). A rolling buffer of ~34 rows is redrawn once per row; the
  smooth upward scroll is a per-frame `translate` on one `<g>`, seamless across
  the buffer shift.
- **Violet ramp** (`violet-100 … violet-800`): the treble (bell 1) is brightest,
  the tenor deepest. The currently-sounding bell glows; a soft radial bloom rises
  whenever the ring returns to rounds.
- The braid **autostarts silently on load** and animates on its own — a muted
  glance shows it alive and evolving with zero interaction.

## Tuning (audio)

Each bell is a tuned **tower-bell voice** (`audio.ts`): the classic bell partials
— **hum** (½), **prime** (1), the characteristic minor-third **tierce** (1.2),
**quint** (1.5), **nominal** (2) and two faint upper partials — as lightly detuned
sine/triangle oscillators under a long exponential decay, giving the inharmonic
"clang → hum" of a cast bell.

The bells are tuned to a **just-intonation diatonic scale** with the tenor as the
tonic (D3 ≈ 146.83 Hz), so the ring sounds a descending major scale rather than a
C-pentatonic cliché. The bells are struck in the exact order of each successive
row, so you *hear* the changes cascade and re-weave. Underneath sits a low
just-intonation **drone bed** (`_shared/psych/droneBank`) rooted an octave below
the tenor, and the whole peal blooms through a generated **convolution reverb**
(`_shared/psych/convolutionVoid`) — the tower. No percussion, no melody line.

## Interaction (keyboard + pointer)

- **Space** — pause / resume the ring.
- **Method switch** — Plain Bob Minor (6) vs Triples (7); **plain course** vs
  **bob touch**.
- **Follow a bell** — click a numbered swatch (or press its digit `1`–`7`) to
  light that one bell's blue line brighter and dim the rest.
- **Tempo** — a speed slider (each row over ~1–3 s; bells ~120–250 ms apart).

## Safety

- **Strobe-safe:** motion is slow and continuous; the only luminance change is the
  gentle "home" bloom, which swells/decays over ~1.4 s (well under 3 Hz). No
  flicker.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion`** (via `_shared/psych/safeFlicker`): the smooth
  upward scroll is disabled — rows step forward on commit instead.
- **AudioContext** is created/resumed only inside the "Start sound" gesture
  (autoplay policy / iOS). Visuals never depend on audio.
- If Web Audio is unavailable a `text-destructive` notice appears and the braid
  keeps ringing in silence — never a white screen.
- Full teardown on unmount: rAF cancelled, all audio nodes stopped/disconnected,
  `AudioContext.close()`, listeners removed.

## Self-assessment

**What works:** the method engine is genuinely correct — verified permutations and
verified return-to-rounds for both stages and both call plans — which was the
headline ambition. The blue-line braid is a true rendering of that data in pure
SVG, it autostarts silently, and the tuned inharmonic bell voices struck row-by-
row over a drone give the intended meditative, non-repeating cascade. Stage
switch, plain/bob touch, follow-a-bell, tempo and space-to-pause all work, and the
"home" bloom rewards the moment the extent comes round.

**What's unverified without speakers / a display:** the actual *timbre* balance of
the bell partials and the drone level (tuned by ear-in-head, not on real
monitors); whether the reverb wetness feels cavernous or muddy on good speakers;
and how the braid's meet-scaled ribbon reads on very wide vs very narrow viewports.
The `droneBank` has no re-root, so on a stage change the drone stays at the
6-bell tonic while the bell voices retune — close enough across these two stages
but not perfectly re-pitched.
