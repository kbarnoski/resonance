# Ledger

**Status**: demoable

What if the world's live financial trades — the mundane torrent of money changing hands right now — conducted one of Karel's real piano recordings? Ledger takes a live tape of real market matches from Coinbase Exchange's keyless public market-data WebSocket (`wss://ws-feed.exchange.coinbase.com`, the `matches` channel for BTC-USD / ETH-USD / SOL-USD; WebSockets bypass CORS) and uses it to granularly re-voice ONE of Karel's real piano takes (default "Rolling", any Welcome Home / Snowflake track selectable). Every incoming trade fires one grain — a short slice of the decoded real buffer, never a synth (house rule 10) — and a slow half-speed bed loop of the same take holds underneath so it stays musical between trades. The mappings: **buy vs sell** plays the grain a few semitones higher vs lower (playbackRate) and pans right vs left (StereoPanner); **trade size** (USD notional, log-scaled) sets grain loudness, grain length, and each glyph's brightness and scale; **short-term price velocity** (the smoothed rate of change across recent trades) drifts the overall transpose and opens or closes a shared lowpass — a rising market lifts pitch and brightens, a falling one darkens and drops. This is the timbre-by-volatility idea from current market-sonification practice, but the sounding body is always Karel's piano, transformed, never a tone generator. The visual is a warm horizontal river of transaction glyphs in three.js — trades enter from the right and drift left as glowing money-as-light on a candlelit gold/amber-into-violet near-black field, size by trade size, vertical position by price level within each product's lane, hue warm/bright for buys and dim/violet for sells, with a bloom pulse driven by the shared safeMaster analyser. It is not a chart; it is a living river. Because the sandbox is headless and a review may be offline, a self-contained synthetic tape (a plausible random-walk price with Poisson-timed buys/sells and a slow meandering regime) runs on mount and keeps running until the real socket delivers its first message — the mono status line reads `demo · synthetic tape` and flips honestly to `live · coinbase` only when a real trade arrives — so the piece looks and sounds complete on a cold, offline, muted phone before anyone taps Begin. Every grain, the bed, and the tone filter terminate in one `createSafeMaster`, never `ctx.destination`; polyphony is capped, and there is a DOM-ticker fallback if WebGL is unavailable. It deepens the market-sonification lineage — **Jordan Wirfs-Brock, "Sounds of a Volatile Stock Market"**, and the real-time trade sonifiers in the Marketbuzz / sMax line — by replacing their synthetic tones with grains of Karel's real recorded piano.
