## New since yesterday
- **[15920-duetlink](https://getresonance.vercel.app/dream/15920-duetlink) — two people conduct one of your takes together.** Your single real recording is split (Linkwitz-Riley ~420 Hz crossover) into a **LOW voice** and a **HIGH voice**. The **host** conducts the low voice, the **guest** the high — each person's cursor sweeps their voice's colour + stereo pan (x) and gain + a gentle ±2.5% tempo lean (y). Both peers see both cursors and hear both voices, sample-close in time, as two intertwining WebGL2 ribbons in a cold blue/white palette (a deliberate break from the warm-ember rut of the last three). **Why open it:** it's the lab's **first multi-user prototype** — a genuinely never-run subsystem (WebRTC), and the exact 4/5 your jury named first: "two people conducting one take together, each drives a voice… NOT a fourth pair of solo hands." It elevates the conduct→canon lineage from two of your *hands* to two *people*. **To feel it solo:** just open the page in **two browser tabs** — they pair instantly over a same-browser channel (one becomes host/low, one guest/high). Alone in one tab, an auto-partner conducts the other voice so you still hear the full duet. **To play it with a real friend:** the "connect a friend" panel gives you a copy-paste code (real WebRTC data channel, no server) — you send yours, they send back theirs, and you're conducting the same take from two machines.

## Research findings worth a look
- The dive (RESEARCH §2026-08-24): the 2026 browser-audio consensus for collaborative sound is **don't stream the audio between people** — hold the recording on both sides and sync only a *shared transport position*, NTP-style over a WebRTC data channel (ping/pong → correct for half the round-trip; anchor `start(when, offset)`; re-anchor every couple seconds to stop drift). Networked-music-performance research says small, bounded timing offsets are musically fine — which is what makes a serverless peer-to-peer duet feasible at all. **Honest caveat:** arxiv's listing/API endpoints were unreachable from the build box tonight, so I anchored on the verified engineering consensus rather than cite an unverified "fresh" paper — I won't fabricate a source.
